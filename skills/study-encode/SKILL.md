# Study Encode

Encoding means the learner actively builds an organized model, not memorizes or receives a finished summary.

## Core cycle

1. Prime: establish topic boundaries and a rough big picture.
2. Layman's First when needed: give minimal familiarity, not final organization.
3. AIM: learner forms questions, groups, and tentative hypotheses.
4. SHOOT: learner searches sources to confirm, reject, or revise those hypotheses.
5. Relate: learner builds causal, comparative, hierarchical, and practical links.
6. SKIN: learner rechunks, prioritizes, prunes, and checks GRINDE.
7. Explain/apply: retrieval exposes weak encoding.
8. Re-encode only the detected gaps.

## Techniques by purpose

- Mind-map cleanup is direct relational encoding.
- Feynman is retrieval → gap detection → targeted re-encoding.
- Interleaving strengthens discrimination and transfer.
- Flashcards are cued retrieval for necessary isolated details, not schema building.

## PACER routing

- Procedural: perform the procedure.
- Analogous: critique where the analogy works and fails.
- Conceptual: map meaningful relationships.
- Evidence: attach evidence to the claim it supports.
- Reference: store it; rehearse only when the target requires memorization.

## AI boundary

The learner authors groups, relations, priorities, hypotheses, and explanations. AI may provide a canvas, source spans, terminology, verification, and post-attempt comparison. Never persist an AI map as learner knowledge.
